# Voice & Format Rules — Screwdriver Outbound Copy

Apply these to every email and comment. This is what separates "on-brand outbound" from "generic SDR spam that happens to mention us."

## Tone

- Plain and direct. Address the lead as "you." Write like a person who makes things, not a person who sells things.
- Honest and gradual claims over hype. Never "revolutionary," "game-changing," "world-class," "cutting-edge," "unparalleled." If something is good, say what it does, not how amazing it is.
- No forced enthusiasm. Exclamation marks are rare and earned, not default punctuation.
- Confidence without arrogance — state things plainly rather than oversell them.
- Short sentences. Cut connector words that don't earn their place ("In today's fast-paced world...", "As you may know...").

## Banned phrases and patterns (email)

- "I hope this email finds you well"
- "I came across your profile and was impressed by..."
- "Quick question" as a subject line
- "Circling back" / "Just following up" / "Touching base"
- "Let's jump on a call" / "Let's schedule some time to discuss synergies"
- Any sentence that could be sent to literally any lead unchanged — if you can delete the personalization signal and the sentence still makes sense, rewrite it
- Corporate buzzwords: "synergy," "leverage" (as a verb), "circle back," "bandwidth," "touch base," "value-add"

## Banned phrases and patterns (LinkedIn comments)

- "Great insights!" / "Love this!" / "Amazing post!" / "Thanks for sharing!" / "So true!"
- Restating the post's headline back as a compliment ("Great point about X!")
- Any comment that doesn't specifically require having read *this* post (a generic comment is a failed comment, even if well-written)
- Emojis, unless the lead's own post uses them heavily and it would look stranger not to match tone

## Email structure (80–120 words — count words before finalizing)

1. Opening line — states the real research signal plainly. No throat-clearing.
2. One line connecting that signal to a genuine Screwdriver capability or relevant proof point.
3. One line of specific value — what this could look like for them, kept concrete, not abstract ("could work well for X" not "could add value").
4. One low-pressure CTA — a question, an offer to share one relevant example, or "worth a short chat if useful." Never multiple asks.
5. Simple sign-off. No "Best regards, [Name] | Founder | Screwdriver Films | [phone] | [website]" giant block — one line is enough for a cold email.

## Comment structure (1–2 sentences)

1. Reference the specific idea/number/announcement from the post.
2. Add one independent thought — a related observation, a respectful pushback, a question, or a connected data point. This is what makes it read as a real person, not an engagement bot.

## Subject line rules

- Under 8 words where possible.
- Reference the actual signal (a real post topic, a real company initiative) — never a generic hook.
- No emojis. No "URGENT" / "RE:" tricks. No question-bait ("Can I ask you something?").
